## Working title
**What If the Guru Doesn't Find You?** (family option: *The Guru Who Never Came*)

## Thesis
"When the student is ready, the teacher will appear" is the most repeated
spiritual sentence in the West — and it's not Buddhist. The film traces the
trope to its real origin (Theosophy, 1914), then asks the question the trope
avoids: what if you're ready and no one comes? Six interpretations follow,
from the deathbed to the realization that you were always the teacher.

## Banked evidence (from web + library)
- Fake Buddha Quotes (Bodhipaksa): the quote's Theosophical origin (1914,
  Herald of the Star; 1918, Theosophy magazine; 1922, New Age magazine;
  1927, Parchment). The Buddha "stressed how fortunate people are when
  they're able to encounter teachers, not the inevitability of such a
  thing happening."
- Osho, New Alchemy #28 (full text banked in web): "Only the master can
  choose you; you cannot choose him." · "the master is like death" ·
  "Even if you have surrendered to a false master, surrender is helpful."
- Katha Upaniṣad (Nachiketa/Yama): death as the original teacher; three
  days of waiting; the secret of immortality.
- Eklavya (Mahābhārata): ready student, absent guru, stone statue,
  Drona's punishment.
- Buddhist Voice: "The teacher can be in any form, any shape, need not
  even be human."
- Osho, Sword and the Lotus: "the master becomes the guest in the heart
  of the disciple" — upanishad as the master-as-guest-in-the-heart.
