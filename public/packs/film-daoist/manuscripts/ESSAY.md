— DAOIST: THE APPOINTMENT (2:45–5:15)

china holds two answers that refuse each other. philosophical daoism:
death is hua — transformation, the same process as autumn. zhuangzi's
butterfly runs to the end, and his best joke: if your arm becomes a rooster
after death, use it to tell the time. [READ — Philosophy Now, verify trim]:
"Life, in general, goes on, though we may not."

religious daoism wants the other thing: to keep the garment. the xian are
refined off the lot — no dying needed, dragon, noon, witnessed. and the
middle trick: shijie, corpse-deliverance. [READ — Ying Zhou, Penn 2019]:
"seemingly succumbed to death, but what appeared to be his corpse was later
replaced by either a bamboo staff, sword or sandals."

the moment of death on this map is optional — either skipped by appointment
or staged with props. says who: the only system trying to cancel death
entirely AND declaring it nothing, in one tradition.

the counter: the immortal-body track is craving-for-continuation wearing
cultivation robes; the zhuangzi freedom-track leaves unclear whether
butterfly-you is anyone. same satta-problem, chrysalis dress.
