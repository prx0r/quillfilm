# STOP 3 — GREEK (metempsychosis)

## WHAT
The soul transmigrates: Pythagoras remembers his previous lives (the
"exemplary transmigrator" — soldier, boy, courtesan, fish); Plato builds the
judicial version — the soul chooses its next life, is judged after, and the
cycle continues until philosophy purifies it. ORPHIC-PYTHAGOREAN: the fallen
soul climbs back through reincarnations (the gold-leaf tablets = game hints).

## WHAT'S GOING ON
The soul is a pre-existing stranger wedded to body-prison (Phaedo: the body
as tomb, the practising of "dying" = philosophy). Between lives: judgment
(minos/rhadamanthys) + the plain of Lethe (forgetting) before descent —
the forgetting-curse that later monotheisms inherit and the NDE folk-retell.

## THE EXACT MOMENT OF DEATH
Plato, Phaedo 114d (verbatim bank, Jowett/Grube — verify tr. at draft):
"the soul of the dead… being of a certain nature… goes somewhere… [and] the
soul which is pure and has practised philosophy departs to the places akin
to it" — and the moment: the judgment-seat at death (Minos + Rhadamanthys);
for orphans: the immediate flight of the soul to its kindred region.

## LOGICAL ARGUMENT
- **Phaedo**: if the soul were generated with the body, it dies with it;
  but ideas/math (recollection-anamnesis) prove the soul knew before →
  pre-existence → therefore survival (the RECOLLECTION ARGUMENT — the
  western ancestor of the buddhist verification claim: knowledge from
  before you as evidence of before-birth).
- Plato's mystic one-liner (Phaedo 81a): true philosophers practise dying —
  "death is the separation of soul from body" — the whole practice IS
  rehearsing the transfer.

## PRIMACY CLAIM
The oldest philosophical model in the West; the ancestor-source (Orphics →
Pythagoras → Plato → Gnostics → Christians). The map's own root.

## BEST OBJECTION
- Aristotle refuses (soul perishes with the body — De Anima: soul as form)
  — the internal Greek counter.
- The buddhist counter is sharper: Phaedo's soul IS what all six schools of
  v1 spent the film dissolving (SN 44.10 refusal vs Plato's confident
  passenger).
