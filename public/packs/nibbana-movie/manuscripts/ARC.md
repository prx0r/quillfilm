# ARC — narrative scope (the spectator's journey)

The overarching narrative is **about the film itself** — a magic show,
a screen, a voice describing something. The BODY is **a tour of the
motifs** (the anchors). The SUBJECT — never explained — is nibbāna,
which is also, quietly, the spectator. The vibe: the spectator spends
the whole film trying to work out what the narrator is talking about.
At some point a penny *might* drop. If it never drops, the film is
still a beautiful, slightly mysterious object; if it drops, it is a
meditation on their own life. Both outcomes are successes.

---

## Three layers (always running at once)

**FRAME (the movie about a movie).** Hook: a magic show; a voice that
keeps addressing "you" while nominally discussing a screen. Intro: a
book, a chant, a screen made of pixels, the screen that never changes.
Coda: the film decomposes into its own medium and loops to the
question. Everything the spectator thinks is *about the film* is also
a statement about *seeing*.

**BODY (the motif tour).** Twelve anchors with bells, one per scaffold
section as the world is walked: sandcastle · vortex · mirror · chess ·
knots · dog-on-plank · puppets · razor · raft · lotus · fire ·
stilling. Each is self-contained: a complete little film with one
banked line, beautiful without commentary. This is what keeps it from
being "just a very confusing movie."

**SUBJECT (the drop).** Two pennies, optional, never forced:
1. **"…he's talking about me."** — the spectator's own seeing, not the
   screen. First stab: hook H4 (black, audio only, the audience
   described over your dark room). Later carriers: the pixels that
   never change [F3], "Ah, here I am" [F11], the dog seeing because it
   is looking [F12], "a heap of imaginings" [F26].
2. **"…he's explaining nibbāna."** — never announced, never explained.
   The silent tell: the book cover in greyscale (I1–I2) *reads* the
   title. The spoken tell: **once, late** — [F35] at E1, after the
   flame is out: "For, the destruction of craving, Rādha, is
   Nibbāna." After that word, the whole film can be re-heard; before
   it, every zone was just an image.

## Confusion budget (mysterious ≠ meaningless)

1. **Every motif is a complete unit:** one image, one banked line, one
   bell. Even a spectator who understands nothing leaves with twelve
   pictures and twelve sentences.
2. **No zone is empty:** where the bank is thin, write a `[RENDER]`
   from a real line — never invent, never pad with mood-words.
3. **The frame question ("what is he talking about?") is answered
   incrementally, never all at once:** H answers "about seeing"; zones
   answer "about *this* image"; E answers "about nothing on a screen."
4. **The naming is singular:** [F35] once. If a second "nibbāna" line
   appears, the trojan dies.
5. **If a line explains the trick, cut the line** (POINTER law).
