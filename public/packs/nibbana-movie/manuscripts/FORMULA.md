## CONTINUATION (same thread — from your last question)

**but why does it grab things in the first place?**
because it was built to grab BEFORE you decide. feel first, ask later —
that's the rule the body has been running for a million years.
grabbing isn't the malfunction. it's the oldest feature you own.

**ok but why can't it stop?**
because it's never just the grab. the grab grabs ITSELF. you grab the
feeling, then grab the story about the feeling, then grab the you who
has the story. papanca = spiralling = this exactly. each grab is the
next grab's handle. you're not trying to stop one thing — you're
trying to stop a chain that feeds itself.

**so nirvana = ?**
nirvana = the training of the body to destroy that fast grabby thing
and retrain it to stop grabbing things — and then, once the chain
can't feed, nothing. one grab ends, the next never starts, the one
after that has nothing to attach to. the destruction of craving isn't
a feeling you have; it's a chain running dry.

**how does the body learn that?**
the body doesn't learn by being told. it learns by watching the grab
happen and get nothing. grab → nothing. grab → nothing. each time the
body records: didn't need it. that's the practice — that's the whole
path in one sentence. (not belief. repetition with the outcome
removed.)

**then why 33 sermons?**
because the chain has 33 disguises. fire, sandcastle, dog, mirror,
razor, raft… the sermons aren't extra teaching — they're the ID list.
to catch the grab every time it shows up in a new costume you have to
have seen it in every costume. that's why understanding nirvana
requires all of buddhism: not because it's deep, but because the grab
is a shape-shifter and the formula needs every term expanded until you
can spot it anywhere.

**so: nirvana = the training of the body to stop grabbing, until the
chain runs dry. suffering = the chain feeding. craving = the grab.
that's the whole recipe book.**

## THE SUBSTITUTIONS (push the rule until it bottoms out)

nibbāna = **destruction of craving.**
destruction of craving = **the grabbing stops.** A feeling lands —
pleasant, painful, whatever — and the hand does not go out: no
reaching for it, no pushing it away, no story hanging off it.
⟹ nibbāna = feelings still happen; the grabbing doesn't.

destruction of craving = **the pull in things decays out.** The pull
was never out there — your system was adding it (that's what evolution
built it to do). Stop adding it and: the cake is just cake, the insult
is just sound, the phone is a rectangle. The things just stand there.
⟹ nibbāna = the world with the fake-pull switched off.

destruction of craving = **you can't un-see that the pull was added.**
You saw where it came from, so even when it flickers you read it as
added, not as reality. It doesn't come back as belief — because it's
what you know, not how strong you are.
⟹ nibbāna = a seeing that can't un-see. (irreversible by knowledge,
not by willpower)

destruction of craving = **the second layer stops.** No more "and
then"; no feeling bad about feeling bad; no rehearsing tomorrow; no
craving about craving.
⟹ nibbāna = one layer of experience — the feeling, and nothing on
top of it.

destruction of craving = **nobody home to keep anything.** The six
senses still run — seeing, hearing, thinking — but no one is
collecting. Nothing is "mine," so there is nothing to lose, guard, or
dread.
⟹ nibbāna = experience without a keeper.


## STREAM ENTRY, SHARPLY (the point of no return)

Question: is stream-entry a physiological point of no return where the
grab can't get stronger? Answer in three cuts:

1. **What snaps is not the grab — it's the misbinding's owner.**
Stream-entry = the first structural installation of the
view-from-outside-the-binding: the identity-peg (sakkāyadiṭṭhi) that
the grab needed in order to *recruit a self* is cracked. The
tone→urge machinery still fires (even the Buddha was offered
temptations) — but the urge can no longer grow a story: it cannot
recruit the self-model, cannot spiral, cannot build the second dart.
The grab *can* fire; it cannot *escalate*.

2. **Why it's a point of no return:** it's knowledge, not strength.
Habits (strength) can reassert under stress — the tradition even
allows a stream-enterer minor ethical failures — but the *view* cannot
re-form, because the peg it would need to hang on is gone. You cannot
un-see the trick. Optimization terms: not a flattened landscape but a
**revised objective function** — no "my discrepancy" to minimize; the
recursion has no ground to restart.

3. **The ladder = which branches are deleted:**
- **stream-entry** — the recursion re-entry ramp deleted (identity-
  grab can't re-form); the sensual grab may fire but can't recruit a self
- **once-returner** — the sensual branch's charge halved (the tag's
  pull visibly dimmed)
- **non-returner** — the sensual branch **deleted** — no appetitive
  tag for sense-pleasure exists anymore: it *can't* get stronger,
  there is nothing to strengthen
- **arahant** — approach, avoid and model-error branches all gone, and
  the recursion node dissolved with them.
(The canonical guarantee — "cannot fall", ≤7 lives — is the doctrine's
own statement of the same irreversibility; the physiological claim we
may make is the objective-function one.)

## THE SAME STOP, MANY CAMERAS (why every tradition fits)

Order of operations: **define it tight first** (the chain above: the
grab ends, the chain runs dry — one stop, no place, no state added).
Only then look at the traditions. Every one of them is a camera
position on that same stop — never a second object.

| camera | tradition | what it stares at | the method that follows |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the gap** (100ms between feeling and grab) | suttanta minimal / our formula | the event itself | see-grab→nothing, again, across disguises |
| **the stain** (what the grab leaves) | Thai Forest — Ajahn Martin, "sugar-coated poison" | the soiled mirror | purification practice: watch the mind soil, clean it, repeat |
| **the plumbing** (what stores it) | Visuddhimagga / Abhidhamma — the Ñāṇamoli house | leaks (āsava) + buried layers (anusaya) | systematic seven-stage machinery for what's *stored*, not just firing |
| **the lamp's oil** (fuel-relative) | the canon's "two elements" (Sermon 1) | the same stop at two moments: with-remainder / without-remainder | no method — it's the definition: one extinction, oil running or out |
| **one facet each** | the similes: fire / cooling / ocean / shore / stilling | fuel-gone · heat-gone · no-shore · not-crossable · quiet | the image you can actually picture |
| **the tone→grab link itself** | Hillside / "Feelings are Suffering" | why the tone becomes suffering | break the link: the discrepancy is generated, not given |
| **the whole costume factory** | Tsongkhapa's Lamrim (+tantra) | assumes you CANNOT see the 100ms event yet | stabilize the instrument → walk every disguise systematically → dig the buried layers → (tantra) grab→transmuted, not abandoned |

Reading rule: a teaching tells you where its camera sits and how long
it stays there. The stain teachers stay *after* the event; the
machinery teachers map what *keeps* it; the Lamrim repaints the whole
theater because the student can't spot a 100ms event in the dark yet.
**If a teaching ever names a second object, it's wrong. If it names a
different position on the same stop, it's tradition.**
