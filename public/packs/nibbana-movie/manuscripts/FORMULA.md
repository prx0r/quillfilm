## THE CHAIN

**L0.** nibbāna = **the complete end of suffering.**

**L1.** Suffering (dukkha) is not the tone — it's the discrepancy the
grab puts on the tone. The sting + "my sting". Pain stays; the ache
of *carrying* it is what ends. [VALIDATION: two darts, SN 36.6;
Hillside, "Feelings are Suffering": *the 'painfulness' of pain is
suffering and not the pain itself*]

**L2.** Therefore: **nibbāna = the destruction of craving** — because
suffering is craving-made. Not a metaphor: the discrepancy is
*generated by the presence of taṇhā, not by the world* (Hillside;
AVI 63: *the various things just stand there in the world*).
[DECODE: taṇhā = the lean · banked [F35]: "For, the destruction of
craving, Rādha, is Nibbāna."]

**L3.** What is craving? **Not the thing you feel for cake.** It has a
specific definition: a physiological/psychological *response* that
happens just under your conscious awareness after any tone — the fast
grabby thing (25–100ms), often felt as a clench in the body before any
story starts. [VALIDATION: opentheory — Cammarata "~100ms", Ingram
"25–50ms", Stevens "clench/fused to"; Berridge: wanting ≠ liking]

**L4.** So: **nibbāna = the training of the grab** — retraining that
fast grabby thing to stop grabbing. Not killing the feeling system:
inserting awareness into the gap between tone and lean, so the lean
fails or fades. *The sequence feeling→craving is not a necessary one*
(Nyanaponika). Disenchantment → the colour fades (virāga) → the hand
opens. [DECODE: yoniso manasikāra · virāga · the razor — hold it
lightly or it goes blunt (F15)]

**L5.** But **why does it grab in the first place?** Back to the 33
sermons. Because:
(a) there is something to signify: *lust, hate and delusion are
"makers of signs"* — signs become significant only while those three
exist (S25);
(b) the stamp (saññā) is applied so fast it feels like the thing
itself — and the stamp spirals: sign → story → web (papañca, the
spiralling);
(c) the spiral is pegged by the "I" (asmimāna — seeing → "I see");
(d) and the whole thing runs as a loop (vaṭṭa) that splits every
moment into two ends and a middle (S33: *where there are two ends,
there is a middle*) — the grab is the mind trying to close a gap it
itself created; the mirage gap can never be closed by running.
This is **dependent origination**: this-then-that, all the way down.

**L6.** Which means: **to understand nibbāna you basically have to
understand all of Buddhism — and that's literally the point.** The
loop in (L5) *is* the teaching's object. The 33 sermons are nothing
but the unpacking of this right-hand side — every simile (fire, raft,
dog, sandcastle, seamstress) is one substitution of the same
equation. The whole of Buddhism is the formula to attain nibbāna: a
recipe book where instead of a cake you get the complete end of
suffering.

**L7. — the minimal path, fully reduced.** Once L1–L5 are seen, the
path reduces to four moves:
1. **See** that suffering is grab-made, not world-made (right view).
2. **Go to the source** when the tone lands — before the object
   (yoniso manasikāra: not "what is this?" but "where is this coming
   from?").
3. **Watch the grab** until it stops being automatic (sati on the
   lean; the grip loosens = virāga).
4. **Nothing left to grab** — the loop has no fuel: nibbāna.
That is the four noble truths compressed into one sentence: *suffering
is the grab; the grab can end; you can watch it end; that watching is
the way.* The recipe book's whole ingredient list on one line.

> destruction of craving, because suffering is craving. is that clearer?
> but what is craving and how can i destroy it?

> craving has a specific definition and its not the thing you feel for
> cake. its a specific physiological response that happens under your
> conscious awareness, fast grabby thing.

> so nirvana = the training of the body to destroy that fast grabby
> thing and retrain it to stop grabbing things. but why does it grab
> things in the first place?

(Precision notes for renders, not for the owner's words: "under your
conscious awareness" → *just below the threshold / a beat before
awareness* — training is exactly how the threshold moves; and "the
training of the body" → the grab is somatic but its training happens
through seeing, not through force.)
