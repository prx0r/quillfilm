# DECODE — plain-English key to Ñāṇananda (working glossary + journey)

Our working interpretation, derived from the 33 excavations — not a
dictionary, a *translation key*. Every gloss is traceable to a sermon
line. Use it to RENDER: when a scene needs his words in plain English,
map the jargon through here and stay as close to his phrasing as the
English allows. When in doubt, bank the exact line instead.

**Two films share this key:**
- **FILM A — the surgical film:** the direct explanation of what
  nibbāna is. No trojan. Structure = THE JOURNEY below (1→6), his
  words + these renders. Can be short; voice-forward; images minimal.
- **FILM B — the pointer film** (the current SCREENPLAY): the same
  teaching hidden inside a magic show. The trojan. This key is what
  the narrator "means" at every moment.

## THE ONE-PAGE ANSWER

**Nibbāna is the going-out of the reaching.** Not the death of you —
the ending of the *outward lean*. "Craving" (taṇhā) is not primarily a
feeling of wanting; it is the **movement itself**: the almost
invisible micro-step from *contact* to *mine* — taken a thousand times
a day. Feelings keep happening for an arahant — pleasant, painful,
neutral — what stops is **the lean with the feeling**.

**How can you destroy a movement?** Not by fighting it. You see how it
is built: every lean rests on something else (this-then-that), every
thing it reaches for is made of the leaning (signs the mind applies so
fast they feel like the world), and the "I" that supposedly reaches
was added afterwards. See that, and the charm wears off automatically
→ the colour literally fades (dispassion = *virāga* = fading) → the
hand opens. What remains is the same experience, minus the lean. That
minus is called nibbāna. It is not elsewhere. It was the lean that
made an "elsewhere."

## GLOSSARY (jargon → key → lucid, concrete → trace)

### The engine

**taṇhā (craving) — the lean / the reaching.**
Not a monster emotion. The micro-step: the pleasant taste arrives and
something *goes toward it* (grab); the unpleasant sting arrives and
something *pushes away* (kill); the neutral moment arrives and
something *skips over it or worries about it*. Three flavours: craving
for pleasure, craving to *be* (someone, somewhere, some mood), craving
to *not-be* (this feeling, this problem, me). It is also the "and
then" of the mind: one sensation is not allowed to be just itself.
S26/33.

**upādāna (grasping) — the grab.**
The lean hardened into ownership: "MY phone", "MY view", "ME being
right." The moment a thing gets a possessive attached. "Dependent on
grabbing is existence" — what you grab is what you then *are*.
S1/S22.

**bhava (becoming) — leaning into a next moment.**
Not only "a new life": leaning into being-a-someone in the next mood,
the next role, the next breath. Birth happens every time you become
"the kind of person who…". S2/26.

**vedanā (feeling) — the tone.**
The first micro-tone of any contact: pleasant (want-more), painful
(want-gone), neutral (nothing). A weather-report, not a decision. The
lean happens HERE — the tone is innocent; the grab is the crime.
S30/33.

**dukkha (suffering) — the friction of carrying.**
Two darts: the sting (that stays — even for the arahant) and "MY
sting" (the second lean on the sting). Nibbāna removes the second
dart, not the first. S2.

### The making

**saṅkhāra (formations) — the making-activity.**
Everything you're busy building: a plan, a mood, a personality, an
argument, a film. The magician's gadgets. When seen, they become "just
preparations" — a heap of doing with no doer. S26/29.

**saññā (perception) — the stamp.**
"Urgent", "rude", "beautiful", "my" — the mark you apply so fast it
feels like the thing itself. Like notching a tree to find your way
back: the notch is not the tree. S27.

**papañca — spiralling / proliferation / elaboration.**
The stamp kept stamping: hurt → why me → my life → the universe is
unfair → I am the kind of person to whom… Each stamp creates the next.
The screen that "shows" a film because the spectator keeps adding.
S5/12/25.

**maññanā (imagining) — thing-making.**
The act of turning experience into *things*: a sound becomes a
"speaker", a speaker becomes "my neighbour's fault". "In whatever way
they imagine, it turns otherwise." S25/33.

**viññāṇa (consciousness) — the recognizing.**
Not a soul watching. The moment of registering: eye meets form and
something *registers* "green / face / threat". That registering is the
gap — the difference between the two ends. The dog staring at the
water: the *looking* is what creates the "there". S10/25/33.

**phassa (contact) — the meeting.**
The exact instant eye hits form, sound hits ear, thought hits "mine".
Two things meet, a third (the registering) pops. That pop is where the
lean can start. S10/33.

**nāmarūpa — the named-and-shaped world.**
= the world you actually encounter: the mug already wearing "mug", the
email already "urgent", the person already "friend / rude / stranger".
Name = the label-pattern you recognize by; rūpa = the shape/appearance
it wears. NOT "mind and matter" — it's everything you meet already cut
into labelled pieces. The toddler with the rubber ball knows it by
feel before any word — that's nāmarūpa before the label. S1/3.

**loka (world) — your six-sense bubble.**
Not the planet. The only world anyone has ever had: this seeing /
hearing / smelling / tasting / touching / thinking bubble, plus the
labels on it. When the six senses stop, "the world" stops — the cosmos
can keep spinning. S22/23.

### The furniture of the self

**asmimāna (conceit "am") — the "I"-peg.**
Seeing → "I see". Hearing → "I hear". The postscript stapled onto any
experience. The most basic lean; the peg everything else hangs on.
Uproot it and designation has nothing to grip. S10/15/28.

**sakkāyadiṭṭhi — personality-view.**
The standing conviction "I am this body / this feeling / this role",
stapled to any of the five heaps (body, tone, stamp, making,
recognizing). The view that starts every grab. S1/10.

**āsava (influxes) — the seepages.**
Leans so old they run without you: reaching for the phone before
you're awake; the hum of "something's wrong" with no object. The
spices you unknowingly bring to the cinema. S5.

**kamma (action) — the groove.**
Your leaning becomes a groove; grooves steer. Not cosmic reward —
momentum. "You inherit your leaning." S32.

### The world it builds

**māra — the killer = whatever keeps you leaning.**
The form you lean on becomes the killer: the job you wanted kills your
sleep; the self-image kills the evening. "Where there is no eye, you
cannot go, Evil One." S21/24.

**anicca (impermanent) — nothing holds still.**
Because everything leans on other things: the body at 5, the body now;
the mood of this morning; the idea you loved in 2019. Falling-apart is
built into the leaning. S24.

**anattā (not-self) — no boss in the building.**
If the "owner" of the experience is made of the experience (the lean),
then there's leaning, leaning, leaning — not a *leaner*. S1/22.

### The way out (which is a way of seeing)

**yoniso manasikāra — attention to the source.**
When the tone appears, go to where it came from (contact → senses →
lean) instead of to its object: not "what is this?" but "where is this
coming from?" S28.

**sati (mindfulness) — staying / remembering to look.**
The tool that lets you watch the lean without joining it. S4/29.

**virāga (dispassion) — the fading.**
The actual FEEL of the lean ending: the colour drains from the insult;
the argument stops being interesting; the cake is just cake. He literally
says: the earth-element *fades away from the mind*. S28.

**animitta (signless) — subtitles off.**
Any sign ("danger", "treat", "me") loses its catching power;
experience continues without the labels sticking. S23/29.

**suññatā (void) — empty of grabbable core.**
Look for the anger: you find angry-ness, but no angry one. The room is
"empty" of a person being angry — not empty of anger. Empty of the
thing you could grab. S25/33.

**atammayatā — not-made-of-that.**
Blissful meditation and you don't become "a blissful meditator";
insight and you're not "an insight person"; success and no "winner".
Refusing to *become* any state, even the best ones. S25/30.

**saddhā (faith) — the first lean, used then outgrown.**
The initial "maybe there's a way out" that starts the search. A
vehicle, not the destination. "Faith is desire in disguise." S31.

### The result (which is a way of being)

**nibbāna — the going-out of the reaching.**
The lamp when the fuel is gone: nothing "went" anywhere; there's
simply no more burning. The un-prepared (nothing fabricated). The
assetless (nothing to steal). Said once in the film: "For, the
destruction of craving, Rādha, is Nibbāna." S1/19/21.

**nippapañca — un-spiralled.**
Experience with the subtitles off and the web unwound; a nibbāna
epithet. S12.

**arahant — worthy-of-gifts.**
"By giving up all, one becomes worthy of all." Nothing to steal =
fully given. S21.

**Tathāgata — thus-gone.**
The lean is gone; "such". Can't be located ("is / isn't after death?")
because pins are leans. S20/22.

**tathatā / idappaccayatā — thus-ness / this-then-that.**
Things happening cause-by-cause without anyone owning the movie. The
norm the lean violates; the law that frees you when seen. S2/26.

**vaṭṭa — the loop.**
tone → lean → grab → story → self → next tone. Water going round; no
starting point; you don't find its end by travelling — you break it by
seeing. S23.

## THE JOURNEY, PLAIN (the 6 sections decoded)

**1. What nibbāna is.** The going-out of the reaching. Like a lamp when
the fuel is gone: nothing "went" anywhere — there's simply no more
burning. Not annihilation (there was never a fixed you to kill) — the
end of the lean, which is all "you" ever was in operation.

**2. How the mechanism works.** Everything leans on everything else
(this-then-that). You are that leaning, looped: contact → tone → stamp
→ story → "mine" → "me" → next contact. The loop is fast (faster than
any film), which is why it looks solid. The world you lean at is made
of leans.

**3. What keeps it running.** Three habits: **stamping** (saññā),
**spiralling** (the stamp kept stamping), and the **"I"-peg** added
after every experience. Plus the seepages — old leans so automatic
they feel like the furniture of reality. You bring them to every
screening.

**4. What the mechanism builds.** A two-sided world: me / not-me. The
"me" is the other half of the grab — no grab, no grabber. The
observer, the one who "watches", is the last sign. The puppet show has
no puppeteer; there's only the walking.

**5. How to see through it.** Don't fight the lean — watch it at its
source. Watch a stamp being applied. Watch the gap between you and the
thing never close (the mirage). Hold everything lightly, like a razor
— tight, it goes blunt. Use the tools, then set the raft on the bank.
Disenchantment is automatic once you see the gadgets: the charm was
the lean.

**6. What remains.** The same sounds, sights, tones — minus the lean.
Assetless. Not nothing: everything, un-grasped. The dog stops staring;
the water is still water; the fire is out; the lotus is just there.
What was hidden wasn't a thing — it was the absence of the reaching.
That absence has a name, and it is said once: nibbāna.
