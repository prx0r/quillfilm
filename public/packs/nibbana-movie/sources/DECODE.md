# DECODE — plain-English key to Ñāṇananda (working glossary + journey)

Our working interpretation, derived from the 33 excavations — not a
dictionary, a *translation key*. Every gloss is traceable to a sermon
line. Use it to RENDER: when a scene needs his words in plain English,
map the jargon through here and stay as close to his phrasing as the

---

## THE ONE-PAGE ANSWER (read this first)

**Nibbāna is the going-out of the reaching.** Not the death of you —
the ending of the *outward lean*. "Craving" (taṇhā) is not primarily a
*feeling of wanting*; it is the **movement itself**: the step outward,
the glue between experience and "mine", the "and then" that turns a
sensation into a story into a self. Feelings keep happening for an
arahant — pleasant, painful, neutral — what stops is the **lean with
the feeling**.

**How can you destroy a movement?** Not by fighting it. You see how it
is *built*: every lean rests on something else (this-on-that), every
"thing" it reaches for is made of the reaching (signs the mind notches,
spirals, then mistakes for the world), and the "I" that supposedly
reaches was itself added afterwards — the peg, the am. See that in the
seeing itself and the lean loses its grip; disenchantment (the charm
wears off) → the colour fades (dispassion literally = *fading*) → the
hand opens (release). What remains is the same experience, minus the
lean. That minus is called nibbāna. It is not elsewhere. It was the
lean that made an "elsewhere."

## THE JOURNEY, PLAIN (the 6 sections decoded)

**1. What nibbāna is.** The going-out of the reaching. Like a lamp
when the fuel is gone: nothing "went" anywhere — there's simply no
more burning. The wise go out like this flame. Not annihilation
(there was never a fixed you to kill) — the *end of the lean*, which
is all "you" ever was in operation.

**2. How the mechanism works.** Everything leans on something else
(this-then-that). You are that leaning, looped: sensation → tone →
marking → story → "mine" → "me" → next moment. The loop is fast
(faster than any film), which is why it looks solid. The world you
lean at is made of leans.

**3. What keeps it running.** Three habits: **naming** (signs),
**spiralling** (papañca: sign → story → web), and the **"I"-peg**
(asmimāna) added after every experience. Plus the seepages (āsava) —
old leans so automatic they feel like the furniture of reality. You
bring them to every screening.

**4. What the mechanism builds.** A two-sided world: me / not-me. The
"me" is the other half of the grab — no grab, no grabber. The
observer, the one who "watches", is the last sign. The puppet show has
no puppeteer; there's only the walking.

**5. How to see through it.** Don't fight the lean — watch it at its
source (attention by the matrix). Watch a sign being made. Watch the
gap between you and the thing never close (the mirage). Hold
everything lightly, like a razor — tight, it goes blunt. Use the tools,
then set the raft on the bank. Disenchantment is automatic once you
see the gadgets: the charm was the lean.

**6. What remains.** The same sounds, sights, tones — minus the lean.
"Assetless." Not nothing: everything, un-grasped. The dog stops
staring; the water is still water; the fire is out; the lotus is just
there. What was hidden wasn't a thing — it was the absence of the
reaching. That absence has a name, and it is said once: nibbāna.

## HOW TO RENDER (translate line-by-line)

1. Take the exact [F#] line. Underline the jargon. Map each term via
   the glossary. Keep his *images* (lamp, raft, dog, sandcastle) — they
   are already plain.
2. Render toward the lean-metaphor family (reaching/lean/grab/loop/
   spiral/cooling/fading) so the whole film speaks one plain language.
3. Never explain the trojan: renders describe the *mechanism*, never
   "and this is about you."
4. If the render starts inventing, stop — bank the exact line instead
   (SILENT until banked).
