# VALIDATION — does the DECODE hold? (canon · tradition · science)

Claim-by-claim check of `DECODE.md` against (a) the Pāli canon &
standard scholarship, (b) the sermons themselves + the Analayo parallels
banked in pack 53, (c) the scientific literature on craving — including
**opentheory.net** (Michael Johnson / QRI) exactly as the owner pointed.
Canonical locators below are standard textbook refs; the primary
evidence remains the sermons (banked [F#]s) and the Chinese parallels
recorded in the excavations. FoJin spot-checks were run (SN 12.39 six
fields cease confirmed) but its index is Sanskrit-heavy for Pali
formula queries — noted, not relied on.

## The opentheory alignment (as requested)

Source: Johnson, *Principles of Vasocomputation: A Unification of
Buddhist Phenomenology, Active Inference, and Physical Reflex* (Pt I,
2023, upd. 2025) — https://opentheory.net/2023/07/principles-of-vasocomputation-a-unification-of-buddhist-phenomenology-active-inference-and-physical-reflex-part-i
(also the STV primer: https://opentheory.net/2021/07/a-primer-on-the-symmetry-theory-of-valence)

What they hold vs our DECODE:
- taṇhā as **clench / fast grab (25–100ms)** vs our "micro-step lean" —
  same claim, our words.
- The **body-level** reading: the lean *is* (or drives) a physical
  clench (vascular/muscular tension) — this extends our gloss: the lean
  is not only phenomenological but *somatic*. **Adopt as an addendum**
  in DECODE (the lean you can feel in the chest/jaw first).
- Their 2025 correction (taṇhā ≠ upādāna) **validates our separation**.
- Stevens: "we create unpleasantness by identifying with the sensations
  we want and making a commitment to dukkha until we get them" —
  our second-dart gloss, in their words.

## The neuroscience alignment

- **Berridge & Robinson** (incentive salience; Robinson & Berridge
  1993 *Brain Res Rev*; Berridge 2009 *Inquiry*; Tindell et al. 2009
  *J Neurosci* "Wanting what was never liked"): wanting ≠ liking; cues
  become "motivational magnets" (= our signs/nimitta grabbing); addiction
  = wanting without liking (= taṇhā without vedanā — the lean with no
  tone at all).
- **Berridge 2009** even uses the email-tinkling example for a
  cue-triggered urge — the same image as our "phone before you're
  awake."
- **Garland & colleagues, "Craving to Quit"** (PMC3434285): mindfulness
  targets exactly the vedanā→taṇhā junction; Nyanaponika's "not a
  necessary sequence" = the whole practice rationale.

## Nuances to keep (so we stay honest)

1. **Don't collapse taṇhā into upādāna.** The lean (impulse) and the
   grab (clinging) are consecutive links; several modern writers and
   the opentheory correction itself warn about this conflation.
2. **"Movement not feeling" is interpretation** — faithful to the DO
   structure + neuroscience, but some trad-English renderings say
   "desire"; our key is allowed its reading *because* the canon's own
   chain separates tone from craving. Labelled, not hidden.
3. **atammayatā and vasocomputation are extensions** — one is
   Nāṇananda's distinctive etymology, the other a speculative scientific
   bridge. Both flagged in-file; neither may be taught as "the canon
   says."
4. **The two-arrow simile is canonical, not from the sermons** — cite
   SN 36.6 if used in a film; don't attribute it to Ñāṇananda.

## The other two houses (how *they* explain it all)

### House 1 — Bhikkhu Ñāṇamoli (1905–60, BPS; translator of the
Visuddhimagga as *The Path of Purification*)
The classical scholastic account. In his *Three Cardinal Discourses*
essay:
- **Nibbāna** = "extinction… of the fires of lust, hate, and
  delusion, or, more briefly, of craving and ignorance"; the nir+vana
  etymology; the smith's fire that goes out when the bellows stop; the
  lamp through exhaustion of wick and oil. "**Clinging's ending is
  nibbāna.**"
- **Caution he forces on us:** "though the word *taṇhā* doubtless once
  meant 'thirst' (Skt. tṛṣṇā) **it is never used in Pāli in that
  sense**." Our glossary's lean/reach reading is safe (it doesn't rest
  on the thirst image), but "thirst" must stay *etymology-only* in
  DECODE renders — the Pāli *tasina*-forms (SN 45.170) do exist and
  are translated "thirst" there, which is the legitimate canonical
  hook. (Noted as nuance; no DECODE change needed beyond keeping
  "thirst" out of film renders.)
- The Visuddhimagga's dependent origination is "the working economics
  of the first two truths (suffering as outcome of craving, and
  craving itself)" — the full feeling→craving matrices per becoming
  (one kind of feeling conditions one group of craving, etc.). Our
  "tone vs lean" split is the sutta-level version of this scholastic
  machinery.
- Compactness (saññā) conceals not-self (Vism. Ch. XXI) — our
  stamp/label gloss.
- Nibbāna cannot be identical with, apart from, inside, or "mine" for
  a self (his MN 1 note) — our "not a place" law.

### House 2 — Hillside Hermitage (Ajahn Nyanamoli Thero; Samanadipa,
Slovia/Sri Lanka; the reddit-routed "Teachings of the Middle Way")
Suttanta-purist, Early-Buddhism-only, anti-commentarial on several
points. Their talks map one-to-one onto our glossary (Significant "I
am" · Proper attention · Designation · Craving · Feelings and the
thought). The load-bearing text for us: **"Feelings are Suffering" by
Bhikkhu Ninoslav Ñāṇamoli** (hillsidehermitage.org):
- "the 'painfulness' of pain is suffering and **not the pain itself**."
- "It is because of taṇhā that our experience of pain is painful."
  Pleasant feeling + craving-for-more → "the present pleasant feeling
  appears as inadequate, a lack"; neutral feeling + not-recognized →
  suffering (M 44: "Neutral feeling is pleasant when known, unpleasant
  when not-known").
- The discrepancy "is actually being constantly generated by the
  presence of taṇhā, **and not by the various things in the world**."
- Their clincher verse (AVI 63): "**Thought and lust are a man's
  sensuality, not the various things in the world; the various things
  just stand there in the world**; but the wise get rid of desire
  therein."
- "one's experience of pain is not the reason for one's suffering. It
  is rather the presence of craving, in one's experience, that
  suffering is there."

**Verdict:** House 2's essay is our DECODE's second-dart/tone-vs-lean
chapter, paragraph for paragraph, from an independent living tradition
— the strongest external confirmation in this whole validation pass.
House 1 confirms the classical spine (extinction, clinging's ending,
economics of the truths) and gives us the one genuine correction:
keep "thirst" as etymology and *tasina*-context only, never as the
default render of taṇhā.

## Bottom line

Every load-bearing gloss in DECODE survives three-way contact: the
canon's own structure, the sermons' usage, and the modern science of
wanting/liking and urge-dynamics — with exactly two flagged extensions
(atammayatā's etymology, the vasocomputation somatic addendum). The
decode is safe to build Film A on.
