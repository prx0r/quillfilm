# STOP 1 — HINDU (Vedic/Upaniṣadic)

## WHAT
Ātman — a real self — transmigrates. Bodies are garments; the dressed one changes.

## WHAT'S GOING ON
The self (ātman/jīva) leaves the dead body and, steered by karma, enters
another. At the cosmic scale: the Chandogya's two paths — those with merit
go to the moon (punar-manuṣya: become men again), those without go down to
the plants, to be eaten. The whole round is ran by accumulated action.

## THE EXACT MOMENT OF DEATH
- Breath suspends, prāṇa withdraws to the heart-center; the Vedic ritual
  death-moment = when the udāna frees itself upward (Bṛhadāraṇyaka's death-
  bed "Om" going up). For the film: "the yogi exhales upward and steps off"
  — verify exact BAU IV.4.5 passage at draft.
- Folk timing: the moment of last breath + the soul's departure image
  (soul as rider leaving chariot — Katha Upaniṣad!).

## LOGICAL ARGUMENT (verbatim bank)
- **Gītā 2.22** (IIT Kanpur/Swami Gambirananda translation, verified): "As
  after rejecting wornout clothes a man takes up other new ones, likewise
  after rejecting wornout bodies the embodied one unites with other new ones."
  (Sanskrit: vāsāṁsi jīrṇāni… dehī) — the film's best garment image anywhere.
- **Gītā 2.13** (for the chain): "As are the old, so are the new; as a young
  man casts off old clothes and puts on new ones, so the embodied self casts
  off old bodies and enters new ones" (verify chosen tr. at draft — 3
  translations banked in search results).
- Chāndogya moon-path (5.10.8): "Those who know the sacred texts… go by the
  bright path; those who seek pleasure go by the smoke path" — verify final
  wording at draft (Panchavimsha relation).
- Katha Upaniṣad: the "chariot of the body" — self as passenger (pairs with
  our chariot motif from the probe — SAME image, opposite use: Hindu says
  the passenger persists; buddhist/nanavira says designation only).

## PRIMACY CLAIM
The oldest continuer: the Gītā is scripture, not philosophy-notes — and the
garment model is the intuitive winner (nobody has a better everyday image).

## BEST OBJECTION
- The self-that-wears-garments IS the buddhist target: the whole v1 film
  argues the passenger is a designation (SN 23.2). The objection writes
  itself from our own QUOTES.md — fair fight.
- Problem of evil/karma of the unchosen: why does the passenger keep getting
  bad garments? (Upaniṣadic answers vary — bank at draft.)
