SN 36:21 To Sivaka (Thanissaro, dhammatalks.org — fetched 2026-10-20)

Wanderer: "there are some contemplatives & brahmans who are of this doctrine & view: Whatever an individual feels — pleasure, pain, neither-pleasure-nor-pain — is entirely caused by what was done before."

The Buddha: "There are cases where some feelings arise based on bile… based on phlegm… based on internal winds… a combination of bodily humors… from the change of the seasons… from uneven care of the body… from harsh treatment… from the result of kamma… So any contemplatives & brahmans who are of the doctrine & view that whatever an individual feels is entirely caused by what was done before — slip past what they themselves know, slip past what is agreed on by the world. Therefore I say that those contemplatives & brahmans are wrong."

"old and new kamma do not override other causal factors operating in the universe — such as those recognized by the physical sciences — but instead find expression within them… the Buddha's teaching on kamma avoids determinism." (Thanissaro intro — read the intro note on screen or as DIR.)
