## Thesis (candidate)

Existence has a structure before it has things: the static aspect shows the
framework (o and x, the possibility of plurality), the dynamic aspect shows
the movement (intention, reflexion, the arising of the "observer").
Understand the frame and the whole of dependent origination follows.

## Spine (from the Notes, in order)

1. **Static I**: o represents a thing; a second thing needs x; spatial
   separation in the representation is not part of what is represented.
   Parmenides anchor: what can be thought/said is.
2. **Static II**: the framework is possibility, not inventory — actual things
   are evidence that things can exist.
3. **Dynamic I**: intention (cetanā) as the movement within the frame;
   reflexion splits observer from observed.
4. **Dynamic II**: the five aggregates as the working-out of the structure;
   nāma-rūpa + viññāṇa as the two bundles of reeds (Sāriputta simile —
   shared with the fire-simile vortex).
5. **Bridge**: Nanavira's static/dynamic ↔ Ñāṇananda's whirlpool between
   consciousness and name-and-form (Sermon arc, Mind Stilled).
