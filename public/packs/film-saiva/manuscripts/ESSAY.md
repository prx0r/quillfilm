## B2 / CH.2 — THE TWO LAYERS (1:15–3:00, act I)

what they believe, compressed: there is no soul commuting between bodies.
what continues is the act of recognition itself — īśvara-pratyabhijñā:
the divine self recognizing itself, life to life. samsāra is not a transit
system; it's recognition-FAILURE: you keep meeting your own experience and
not seeing it's yours. liberation is recognition-success.

so the film's question flips: not "what travels after death" — "what would
have to be true for you to recognize anything at all, ever?" and their
answer: [READ — Ratié excavation, Utpaladeva]: every cognition is
"simultaneously consciousness-of-object and a self-manifestation" — it is
"svaprakāśa (self-luminous): just as light reveals objects because it
manifests them by manifesting itself."

## B3–B6 / CH.3 — THE PROOF (3:00–9:15, acts I–II)

> B3 (3:00–5:00): question-flip lead-in · B4 (5:00–6:45): AXE beat, gag
> then dissolve · B5 (6:45–8:00): three powers, three clicks · B6 (8:00–9:15):
> felt-surprise — the film's ONLY fast montage, 5 faces ≤2s, one hit.

the argument, in three moves, in their own words.

one: awareness never arrives second. [READ — Ratié, on Nyāya]: "an
instrument cannot apply to itself (an axe cannot cut itself); a cognition is
known only by a subsequent cognition." the buddhists accepted this; the
nyāyikas used it to deny self-knowledge; śaiva accepts the premise and
keeps the conclusion: consciousness DOES light itself — otherwise the axe
regress never ends.

two: synthesis needs a subject. [READ — Ratié outline of ĪPK ch.2-3]: "the
Self is needed for cognitive synthesis (memory, anusandhāna)" and the three
powers — "jñāna–smṛti–apohana" (knowing, remembering, forgetting) unify
across time — without a unifier, memory is just another orphaned object.

three: recognition is felt, not inferred. [READ — Ratié]: "It always takes,
even fleetingly, the form of a surprise, for we then suddenly realize what
we knew until then without knowing it: that *that*, is me."

and the buddhists saw the same light and drew the other conclusion:
[READ — Ratié]: the śaiva "accepts the Buddhist self-luminosity and the
non-positional reflexivity, but rejects the conclusion that there is no
Self." the whole disagreement sits inside one shared observation.

## B8 / CH.4 — THE EXACT MOMENT OF DEATH (12:30–16:00, act III — four layers, four clicks)

the same discipline as the buddhist cut: three layers, their words, no fog.

one — the prāṇa mechanics. [READ — Tantrasāra, tr. (in house, primary)]:
"The movement of udana proceeds from the heart up to the dvadasanta, a center
of prāṇa twelve fingers above the head." time itself rides these breaths:
the yogin watches "the entire measure of time from one tithi to sixty years
in the single movement of udana." so death, mechanically: the breath-tide
runs up and out to its station above the skull — and everything the breath
was carrying goes with it.

two — the tattva-retraction. the cosmology runs backwards on itself.
[READ — Tantrasāra]: "As [the adhvas] proceeded from consciousness, they
are to be dissolved into it by involutionary methods" — the thirty-six
tattvas collapse in ladder-order back toward Paraśiva: world → time →
māyā → pure-knowledge → Śiva. the exact moment of death = reverse-evolution:
the universe this person was wearing retracts, segment by segment.

three — the ritual form, which is weirder than the philosophy. [READ —
Tantrasāra ch.16]: "the initiation of one who is not present… for the
person who is dead" — the teacher initiates the corpse. and the grace-
grading of the moment: [READ]: "he becomes Śiva after he drops his body"…
"becomes Śiva at the time of dropping his body"… or, the slow grade: "he
attains Śivahood only after experiencing enjoyments in another body after
dropping the present one." there it is — rebirth, in their own sentence:
another body is what slow grace buys you. and for the right death: [READ]:
"the death of such a person is a 'day of festival' (parvādinam) for the
disciples."

four — and the destinations, because every tradition grades the afterlife
and this one grades it four ways. [READ — Tantrāloka 10.28, via in-house
deepdive extract, library/tantra-death]: sālokya — "sharing the world" of
the deity, for the uninitiated who die in sacred places; sāmīpya — nearness,
for initiates who die in sacred places; sārūpya — similarity of form, for
those who meditated on the deity's form; sāyujya — conjunction, the merge
with Bhairava, "virtually equivalent to liberation." and who gets which
depends on initiation-grade: samayin, putraka, sādhaka — [READ, ibid.]:
"the fate of the soul at death is not uniform — it depends on the level of
initiation, the knowledge, and even the place of death." note the map:
sacred-place matters here exactly as Kashi matters in the Hindu film.

## B8b — THE STATION: VARANASI (16:00–18:00, act III location beat — owner: kashi lives HERE, not in the special)

and because "the place of death matters" in this system, there is one
coordinate where the whole doctrine has a street address: kashi.
[READ — Sand 2010]: "Having arrived at the god Viśveśvara one is not born
again." · "If an intelligent man does not want rebirth again on earth, he
should go to Avimukta which is frequented by gods, ṛṣis and gaṇas." the
moment, in their own text: "at the time of death people lose their memory
and in the moment that the soul departs from the body Viśveśvara… confers
upon it a special mantra (tāraka brahma) by which the deceased becomes
[liberated]." — the god meets the departing soul at the exit-station with
a key-phrase.

this is where sālokya/sāmīpya gets its geography (dying in sacred places),
where the perpetual fire at manikarnika never cools — the doms staff the
entry point around the clock — and where the skull-crack rite marks the
exact release of the prāṇ. the śaivite film's dominant image gains its
ground: the mirror stands on a ghat.

## B9–B10 / CH.4b — METAPHYSICS + THE CLIMAX (18:00–22:00, act III)

> Rhythm: 2s hold after "death is not a prerequisite for final release" ·
> white-flash + 2s black at B10's "I am not dying."

now the part that answers the film's actual question — why does this system
produce rebirth AT ALL if recognition is possible without dying?
[READ — Abhinavagupta, Paramārthasāra 16, primary+Lidke]: "Consciousness,
from the influence of possession by Māyā, becomes the tainted soul, the
fettered beast." the body is not the prison: [READ — Lidke on PS]: "the
body itself is not the cause of bondage. It is the final product of
Paramaśiva's expansive power. The true source of bondage is the malas."
and the process-image: [READ — PS 32]: "Like a spider with its web, [the
finite soul] spins for itself a variegated pattern by means of association
with the body, breath, perception, thought, knowledge and the expanse of
ether."

so the loop repeats because the knots (anava, māyīya, kārma) keep spinning
the spider — and the same text delivers the film's strangest answer to
"must you die to be free?": [READ — Paramārthasāra, Lidke]: "for the Trika
śaivite, death is not a prerequisite for final release (videha-mukta)" —
embodied liberation, jīvanmukta, while the heart still beats. every other
tradition in this series schedules death as the arena. this one takes the
deadline off the table. and the cold-open mirror gets its source: [READ —
PS 9]: "As the face is revealed in a spotless mirror, so This (Self) is
revealed as light in the mind purified by śaktipāta."
