## OBJECTION 1 — "each moment you are actively taking yourself out of a
state of jhana" — is this what the texts actually say, or is it a nice
metaphor?

**The claim (CH.2, owner verbatim):** jhana is perpetually being ruined
by you; just stop doing that.

**What the banked texts say (HH 38hh, Ñāṇamoli):** the hindrances
(avijjā/tanha) actively pull attention — not "you" as a person ruining
it, but specific mental factors (sensual desire, ill-will, sloth,
restlessness, doubt). The five hindrances ARE the ruining mechanism.
So "you" in the owner's phrasing = the hindrances, not a homunculus.

**Resolution:** the owner's line is poetically right and mechanism-
correct at the level the video operates. The bridge text after it
should name the five hindrances as the "you" — otherwise a viewer
thinks it's mystical.

**Honest remainder:** does naming the hindrances make it sound like
"just don't have desire" (the prescriptive trap)? The video must not
preach — the blank-screen beat is the teaching, not the explanation.

enough? Isn't cutting to black for 20s on YouTube a retention disaster?

**The claim (CH.3):** 20 seconds of absolute black.

we could have on the segemtns transcript skip to here for a valuable
lesson") turns the retention problem INTO the content. The chapter
marker joke makes the skip visible, which makes the viewer self-aware,

**Resolution:** keep 20 seconds. The DIR card already notes the chapter
marker. But: check the YouTube chapter timestamp matches (CH.3 starts
at 4:00 — chapter marker must read exactly 4:00, no drift).

**Honest remainder:** mobile viewers may close the app during 20s of
black. Acceptable — the debrief catches them when they scrub back.
