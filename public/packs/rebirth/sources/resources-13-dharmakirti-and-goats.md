## DHARMAKĪRTI (c. 600–660 CE) — the proof
- Work: Pramāṇavārttika, Pramāṇasiddhi ch. (PV II vv. 34–131), the classic
  Buddhist rebirth proof vs the Cārvākas (Ajita's intellectual heirs).
- The argument (Hayes, in repo): mental events must have *mental* causes —
  each cognition requires a previous cognition (samanantara-pratyaya).
  The newborn's first awareness ≠ first: it is like waking from deep sleep,
  caused by a cognition before the gap (Prajñākaragupta's explicit link,
  Franco 1997). Therefore: previous life, or acausal birth — pick one.
- The stakes line (Hayes): no rebirth → nirvāṇa is automatic at death for
  everyone → practice pointless. Physicalism = ethical nihilism (Arnold).
- Occasion: Dignāga's claim that the Buddha *is* a pramāṇa — proved via
  compassion cultivated over many lifetimes (PV II.34: "that which
  establishes the Buddha is compassion that comes from constant practice").
- WHERE IT SLOTS: v1 FAQ/dialectic section — the tradition's best lawyer.
  The FAQ answer to "who says?" is: Dharmakīrti took the materialists to
  court in the 7th century and the record survived (Hayes PDF).
- BEST COUNTER (use it, fairness law): Dan Arnold — Dharmakīrti's argument
  is a de facto dualism and his own dreamless-sleep case *compromises*
  his own immediate-precedence causation (Arnold PDF, in repo). The
  strongest modern objection to the strongest classical defense.

## THE OTHER MISSING GOATS
1. **Vasubandhu** (4th–5th c.) — Abhidharmakośa: antarābhava defense
   (intermediate being = five aggregates, aroused by seeing the parents,
   attaches to womb) + seed theory (the store's ancestor).
2. **Saṅghabhadra** (5th c., Sarvāstivāda) — THE antarābhava hardliner,
   Nyāyānusāra vs Vasubandhu; survives only via Xuanzang's Chinese
   translations (Brewster 2023, in hand). The Theravāda-no-gap vs
   Tibet-yes-gap fight has names and dates: this is the fight beat's
   other protagonist.
3. **Asaṅga** — Mahāyānasaṃgraha identity claim, the single best
   shared-ontology quote we have: ālayavijñāna = the root-consciousness
   the Mahāsāṃghikas call mūla-vijñāna = the bhavaṅga of the Sthaviras
   (via Wikipedia/Rebirth-in-Buddhism sourcing the graha). One concept,
   three schools' names — the table's proof-of-concept.
4. **Sthiramati** (6th c.) — vijñānapariṇāma: rebirth without a self via
   transformation-of-consciousness, vipāka/niṣyanda seeds in feedback loop
   (Szanyi, "Rebirth without a self," philarchive — modern lit).
5. **Śāntarakṣita + Kamalaśīla** (8th c.) — the great synthesisers:
   Yogācāra as provisional, Madhyamaka as final (Yogācāra-Svātantrika-Madhyamaka).
   WHY WE NEED THEM: explains how Tibet legitimately holds BOTH Yogācāra and
   Madhyamaka in the same film — the school-map's own joint.
6. **Dignāga** — the pramāṇa seed Dharmakīrti grew.
7. Modern fairness shelf: **Analayo, "Rebirth in Early Buddhism and Current
   Research"** (304pp PDF, in repo!) — the scholarly standard for what the
   EBTs actually say; **Richard Hayes, "Dharmakīrti on punarbhava"** (14pp);
   **Dan Arnold, "Dharmakīrti's Dualism"** (18pp); Finnigan "Conventionalising
   rebirth" (OUP forthcoming — paywall, noted).
