## I. THE COURTROOM (S16 FAQ — DHARMAKĪRTI BEAT)

**[D1] Dharmakīrti, PV II.34 (via Hayes)** — "That which establishes [the Buddha as a source of knowledge] is compassion that comes from constant practice." — hayes-dharmakirti-on-rebirth.pdf — S16 (the proof's occasion).

**[D2] Dharmakīrti (via Hayes, paraphrase-as-quoted-by-scholar — flag)** — "consciousness is dependent on the body. But that is not correct, because it can be disproved [that the body] is a support [of consciousness]." — ibid. — S16.

**[D3] Dharmakīrti (via Hayes)** — "The continuum of mental causes and effects continues on its own—each moment causes the following moment, and it is this continuity that one can call continued existence… one is not saying that there is a separate entity called continuity that has its own separate causes." — ibid. — S16 (his no-self answer, in his own words).

**[D4] Hayes (the stakes)** — "if there is no rebirth, then the very goal of attaining nirvāṇa, understood as the cessation of rebirth, becomes almost perfectly meaningless. Or rather, nirvāṇa comes automatically to every living being that dies, regardless of how that being has lived." — ibid. — S16 (why the tradition had to fight).

**[D5] Dan Arnold (the counter)** — Dharmakīrti's sleep-case "compromises precisely the intuition behind the Buddhist appeal… to the 'immediately preceding moment of awareness' (samanantara-pratyaya) — that this shows, in other words, how difficult it is to defend a reductionist account that… takes mental events to be explicable entirely in terms of efficient, local causation." — arnold-dharmakirtis-dualism.pdf — S16 (fairness law: best objection to the best defense).

**[D6] Dan Arnold (steelman)** — "persons consist simply in causally continuous series of events, and… the series of mental events, insofar as it continues after the death of the body, has indefinite temporal extension. It is, indeed, just the post-mortem continuity of any series of mental events that is called 'rebirth.'" — ibid. — S15/S16 (modern analytic formulation of our whole film).
