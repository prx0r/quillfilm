# RESOURCE — PURE LAND (vow school)

## Mechanism
Dharmākara's 48 vows (esp. 18th): birth in Sukhāvatī assured to one who hears
the Name and entrusts. Death: welcome party + lotus platform; nine grades
(Contemplation Sūtra); lotus opens fast (sincere) or slow (doubtful bud).
Graduation: non-retrogression → buddhahood → return to save others.

## Metaphysics (analytic)
The only school in the set where the carrier is *relational*, not mechanical:
the vow-relationship substitutes for self-continuity. Other-power (tariki)
vs self-power (jiriki) = a soteriology that relocates causation from the
agent to the vow (Tanluan's Easy Path: sailing vs walking). Grades preserve
karma's moral logic inside grace — the ledger still counts, it's just
cosigned. Vasubandhu's Treatise keeps the no-self frame: "the borrowed-term
'person'… neither entirely identical nor completely different" — the only
Pure Land text that states the metaphysics in the set's shared terms.

## Quotes (verbatim, cite)
- Three Pure Land Sutras (BDK, in library): Larger Sutra Part Two — those
  who "h[ear] his name and w[i]sh to be born" are born "by the power of that
  Buddha's Fundamental Vows."
- Vasubandhu, Treatise on Rebirth (Tanluan commentary, in hand via search):
  "I single-mindedly entrust my life to the Tathagata of Unhindered Light…
  May I be reborn in the Land of Peace and Joy." + the neither-identical-
  nor-different person.
- Contemplation Sūtra nine grades: high/middle/low ×3; deathbed welcome
  scenes per grade (pure-land-buddhism.com extracts, in treatment).
- Fugen karmic-hindrance line (Kiss 2026): pre-sectarian pluralism — "before
  it was a sect, it was a crowd."

## Best objections
- If grace guarantees birth, why practice? (The school's own answer:
  shinjin IS the practice; the vow doesn't reward merit, it meets entrustment.)
- The strongest internal objection: lotus-bud delays (doubtful still in the
  reward land debate — Hōnen vs Shinran vs Benchō, in treatment).
