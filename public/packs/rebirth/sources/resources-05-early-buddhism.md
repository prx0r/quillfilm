# RESOURCE — EARLY BUDDHISM (Ñāṇavīra + Ñāṇananda)

## Mechanism
No thing travels: experience is intentional; subject/object co-arise
(Fundamental Structure); "being" = caught-up-in-craving (SN 23.2); rebirth =
the vortex re-forming wherever craving re-grips (nāma-rūpa ↔ viññāṇa, the
reeds). The fire sermon IS the mechanism: upādāna = fuel = grasping.

## Metaphysics (analytic)
Structure-first: existence has a framework before it has contents (static
aspect: o/x possibility; dynamic: intention/reflexion). The self is the
negative pole of the object — not a thing that persists but a structure that
recurrs. This is the *psychological* reading the v1 cliffhanger ends on:
rebirth is constantly happening; kṣaṇa/santāna (moment/stream) is the time-
doctrine that makes "constantly" literal. Ñāṇananda's vortex: "in so far
does a whirlpool whirl, for the designation of a 'thisness'" (Mahānidāna,
via fire-simile sermon).

## Quotes (verbatim, cite)
- SN 23.2 (Radha, "being"): "Any desire, passion, delight, or craving for
  form… When one is caught up [satta] there, tied up [visatta] there, one is
  said to be 'a being [satta].'"
- SN 12.67 (Sāriputta, reeds): "two sheaves of reeds… stand leaning against
  one another… If one were to pull away one of those sheaves… the other
  would fall."
- Ñāṇavīra, sotāpatti record (27.6.1959): "The clear and stainless Eye of
  the Dhamma arose in me: 'Whatever has the nature of arising, all that has
  the nature of ceasing.'"
- Ñāṇavīra on intention: "All experience, including the arahat's, is
  intentional or teleological" (CtP).
- Ñāṇananda (fire-simile sermon): "the obvious meaning of the term
  'nibbāna' is extinction, i.e. going out of a fire."

## Best objections
- Reading the Canon through a 1960s phenomenological lens (critics:
  reconstruction vs exegesis); the school's small size vs its confidence.
- "Constant rebirth" risks collapsing macro and micro into mush — Thanissaro
  answers this with scale-constancy (process is the constant) — use both.
