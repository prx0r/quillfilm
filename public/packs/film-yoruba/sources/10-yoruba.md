# STOP 10 — YORUBA (the chosen-headed)

## WHAT
You pick yourself before you arrive. At the crossroads (orun), the living
consulted by the àbíkú / ogbanje spirit-cycles — the famous spirit-children
who die and return repeatedly (the parents tie anklets to catch the return —
the FOLK answer to "prove it": physical-trap tests on the reincarnating
child). Odu Ifá/Ori: you chose your own ori (head/destiny) in heaven before
descent — destiny as pre-purchase. Ancestors RETURN PUBLICLY (egungun
masquerade: the dead dance among the living yearly — the only tradition
where the transfer is put on a stage for the whole town).

## WHAT'S GOING ON
- Ori = your chosen head: the self-selection moment BEFORE birth (the film's
  clearest pre-birth *decision* doctrine — who chooses in other maps? NOBODY.
  YOU do, here.)
- The abiku cycle: spirit-children testing the contract (retry-loop until
  they stay); names/rituals/markings break the loop (community-engineering
  the reincarnator to stay).
- Egungun: return-as-celebration; the ancestor remains a community-member.

## THE EXACT MOMENT OF DEATH
- The moment: crossroads-crossing (Esu/Elegba at the gate — the divine
  customs-officer, common to Vodun/Haitian Vodou lineages — flag expansion),
  and (in the abiku case) the CHILD'S deaths: each death is a re-attempt
  at the contract. For adults: the joining of the ancestors (egun) —
  verified by the community, not by doctrine.
- Verify at draft: exact Ifá source-verse for ori-choice + abiku ritual
  specifics (Bascom 1969 / Verger trad. / Titon — pick one careful source).

## LOGICAL ARGUMENT
- The evidential-folk argument: abiku trials (anklets, wood-markings) are
  EXPERIMENTAL — the community runs tests on the returner (the folk
  equivalent of the Pa-Auk past-life checks — pair them).
- The social argument: reincarnation here is infrastructural — ancestors
  vote (the community literally brings the dead back to participate).

## PRIMACY CLAIM
The only map where the pre-birth self is an agent with a MENU — and the only
one where the dead keep civic rights.

## BEST OBJECTION
- External: colonial-era dismissal of abiku (misread as superstition vs the
  actual demographic/medical history — include the modern anthropological
  pushback, bank at draft).
- Internal: if you chose your head, whose fault is suffering? (the karma-
  blame vs pre-choice tension — same problem the Hindu stop has, asked in
  Yoruba dress).
