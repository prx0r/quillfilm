# STOP 2 — JAIN

## WHAT
No soul (jīva is NOT a soul-as-substance) — but karma is REAL MATTER: pudgala
(fine matter particles) sticks to the jīva like dust on a mirror and weighs
it through the six destinies. The heretics of the karma-carrier: only school
where what travels is literally *stuff*, without anyone being a stuff-self.

## WHAT'S GOING ON
Every act deposits karmic pudgala onto the soul-stuff (āsrava → bandha
"binding"). At death the load determines the next embodiment: human, animal,
hell, heavenly, plus the two extras (tiryañc is animal; plus deva/lower +
the special classes — the six destinies: deva, manuṣya, tiryak, naraka,
prākīrṇaka for fallen monks, tiryañc-utsargajī). The soul releases particle
by particle through austerity → mokṣa. The load IS the biography.

## THE EXACT MOMENT OF DEATH
Jain moment: the jīva loosens from the karmic body the instant the body's
vital functions end; the karmic body (kārmaṇa-śarīra — the "karmic sheath")
continues as a shadow-self through the next embodiment. Death = the shedding
of the gross body while the *karmic body persists* — the shadow you can't
outrun. (Verify Tattvārtha Sūtra VII treatment at draft.)

## LOGICAL ARGUMENT
- Tattvārtha Sūtra (Sanskrit "authority"): karma must be substantial to
  retain moral order across lives — intention alone can't travel; only
  matter can carry consequences (bank exact sutta-number + wording at draft).
- Mahāvīra's omniscience claim as evidence: the liberated ones SEE the
  past-births as fact (parallels the buddhist forest verification beat).
- The doctrinal map: Sanjīvānī/kalpavṛkṣa (which karma revives = organic
  and inorganic karma split — the most technical karma-mechanism on earth).

## PRIMACY CLAIM
The only karma that's physically real: "your actions weigh — literally."
Tirthankara lineage + the most rigorous ethical system (ahimsa as physics).

## BEST OBJECTION
- Same charge as buddhism + a new one: substantialism smuggled back in via
  pudgala (the buddhist critic says: you reified the dust).
- Why does fine matter care about morality? (the binding question.)
